# R0 storyboard critic: Core 27s 9:16

Verdict: the arc is right for this buyer. The middle is overloaded and repeats itself, and trust/control gets the least time.

## Ranked problems (most severe first)

1. **Too much text to read on a phone.** At ~4 words/sec, the estimated word counts against what each beat can hold are: B4 ~25 words in 2.0s (holds 8), B5 ~14 in 2.0s (8), B6 ~16 in 2.0s (8), B8 ~26 in 3.0s (12), B10 ~24 in 2.5s (10), B9 13 in 2.0s (8). The film carries about 170 words against a capacity of about 108.
   **Fix:** show source names only, with no sub-labels (B4). Show the six capabilities as icons, with only one or two names readable (B5). Remove B6's headline or its five labels. Remove B9's second line. Use one reading target per beat.

2. **The mechanism is explained three times.** B4–6 (flow diagram), B7 (layers) and B8 (seven-step path) take 12s of the 27. Trust (2s) and deployment (2.5s) are squeezed, even though "keep control" is what regulated buyers care about most.
   **Fix:** cut B8 to three highlighted steps (01 Approved sources → 04 Check permissions → 06 Ground & validate → "Useful output. Human control."), or drop it. Give the ~1.5s you save to B9/B10.

3. **On-screen words not in FACTS.md.** "Your knowledge is everywhere." (B1) is invented. "Approved enterprise data, not open-web search." (B9) is a trimmed paraphrase.
   **Fix:** get client sign-off on the B1 line, or replace it with the verbatim label "YOUR BUSINESS" over the wall and let the visual carry "scattered". In B9, use the verbatim line or remove it (see #1).

4. **The same layout repeats.** B4, B5, B6, B8 and B10 are all stacks or rows of white rounded cards, so the film reads as a scrolled web page. Nothing uses a plan/overhead view.
   **Fix:** render B6 as a radial fan of outputs bursting from the ring (wide). Render B8 as an overhead plan with the pulse tracing a path. Keep the vertical card canvas for B4–5 only.

5. **The deployment beat is overcrowded and missing proof of control.** A diagram, a three-option carousel and a headline in 2.5s can't be read. The control labels the buyer wants (Access policies · Audit controls · Data residency) are left out.
   **Fix:** keep the dashed "YOUR CONTROLLED ENVIRONMENT" boundary with Your data → ring → Private model, and add those three labels. Replace the carousel with one line, "Managed Cloud · Your Cloud · On-Premise / Air-Gapped", or drop the headline.

6. **Signature moment 2 contradicts the beats.** It promises "a single light… arrives at an AI agent at the bottom". The beats instead have multiple pulses, and Workflow Automation is the bottom item. A camera scroll down a list is not strong enough to be a signature.
   **Fix:** use one pulse from Documents → Semantic Search → AI Agents. Hold the camera on that pulse (selected item sharp, neighbours blurred), and show cause → effect: the agent card changes state when the pulse lands.

7. **The CTA competes on the end card.** The tagline (already shown in B3), the wordmark, the button and "Ainfinite AI" are four reading targets.
   **Fix:** drop the tagline. Make the "Pilot with your data" button the largest element (≥70% of the width) and keep it on screen from 24.5s to 27s.

8. **Two "Build once." elements collide (B6→B7).** The flying "Build once." and the headline "Build once. Put intelligence to work." risk showing the same words twice over the 3D.
   **Fix:** the flying "Build once." becomes the first line of the headline. Pre-position it at that exact spot so only "Put intelligence to work." enters.

## What works

- Signature 1 (the wall collapsing into the ring, then into the logo) is strong.
- 3D in B7 is justified: the source (image 2) shows the foundation as a layered stack, so separating the layers explains it. Avoid gaps between slabs.
- B11's lines are verbatim and set up the CTA well.
